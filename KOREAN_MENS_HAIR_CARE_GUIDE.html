Read this first (the honest, gentle bit)

You asked for options without the three big drugs. That's a fair choice. These points will help you spend money on things that have a chance of working.

🧠 What androgenetic alopecia is

  • DHT (a testosterone by-product) slowly shrinks genetically sensitive follicles on the hairline and crown. Hairs get thinner, shorter and lighter until some follicles stop producing visible hair.
  • It is progressive. Korean dermatologists stress that the main problem is thinning, not the strands in the shower drain.1
  • Follicles that have been gone for years rarely come back. Starting early matters more than which product you pick.

🇰🇷 What "Korea takes it seriously" really means

  • Korea has the most intense scalp-care culture in the world: hair-loss shampoos, tonics, ampoules, head spas and scalp clinics everywhere.
  • But Korean men are also the world's most enthusiastic finasteride users. Korea is reported to account for about 40% of global Propecia sales.1
  • Korea's regulator (MFDS) allows hair-loss cosmetics to claim only that they help relieve hair-loss symptoms. In February 2024 alone it flagged 622 illegal or misleading hair-loss listings, and it says no food or supplement is approved to prevent hair loss.4

✅ What drug-free care can realistically do

  • Reduce shedding caused by dandruff, oil, inflammation and breakage.
  • Thicken existing hairs and give modest regrowth (single-digit to roughly 20–40% hair-count gains in the better trials, usually small studies).
  • Slow things down. None of these options has been shown to stop progression the way finasteride does.

🩺 Advice from Korean hair doctors

  • Prof. Lee Ji-su (Boramae Medical Center) says cosmetic care can help, but her worry is people delaying real treatment until much more hair is gone.1
  • Practical takeaway: use the drug-free plan below for 6 months with monthly photos. If your hairline or crown is still clearly worsening, see a dermatologist. You can decide about medicines then, with data.

🌸 How to read the evidence flowers

🌸🌸🌸Good several randomized trials or meta-analyses · 🌸🌸🌸Some one or a few small trials, often industry-funded · 🌸🌸🌸Early brand tests, lab or animal data, or theory only.

"Good" here means good for a non-drug option. The evidence base for finasteride and minoxidil is still much larger than anything on this page.

Exosome & PDRN serums: what's real?

You mentioned plant-based exosome serums, so here's a dedicated look. They're the hottest Korean scalp trend of 2025–26.

🫧 What exosomes are

Exosomes are tiny bubbles (30–150 nm) that cells release to pass proteins and RNA messages to other cells. On labels you will see three kinds:

  • Plant-derived (rose, centella, ginseng, turmeric "Capilia Longa"): used in shampoos and ampoules as cosmetic ingredients.
  • Human stem-cell–derived (adipose, umbilical cord): used in clinics, usually with microneedling.
  • Milk-derived: newer, in some topical gels.

📊 What the studies show

  • Human stem-cell exosomes: a small placebo-controlled trial with microneedling showed better hair density at 12 weeks, and single-arm studies show similar gains. A 2025 review from the International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery called the evidence promising but limited.25
  • Plant exosomes: only pilot studies, and these are hard to interpret because the exosomes were mixed with growth factors and peptides.25
  • Milk-exosome gel: a 40-person randomized trial over 12 weeks reported more growing-phase hairs and thicker shafts than placebo.31

⚠️ Safety and regulation

  • The US FDA states there are no FDA-approved exosome products. It has warned about serious adverse events from unapproved exosome treatments.28
  • Exosome sources and quality are not standardised, so two "exosome" vials can contain very different things.25
  • Topical cosmetic ampoules are low-risk. Injected or needled exosomes should only be done by a dermatologist who can tell you the exact product, its source and its approval status.

🐟 PDRN (salmon DNA)

  • PDRN injections are a Korean favourite for skin. In a Korean study of women with pattern hair loss, 12 sessions of PDRN injections alone raised hair counts by about 18%. Adding PRP improved thickness further.29
  • There is little data for men, and no good data on PDRN in shampoos or rinse-off products.
🌷 Verdict: A plant-exosome ampoule is a pleasant, low-risk tonic. Most of its effect probably comes from the caffeine, salicylic acid, panthenol and anti-dandruff agents it also contains, so compare the full ingredient list against cheaper tonics. Clinic exosome therapy is experimental and expensive. PRP and LLLT have more evidence for the money.

🛍️ Buying an LLLT device in India

  • Prof. Huh's advice from Korea: buy only a device that passed clinical trials and is approved as a medical device (MFDS in Korea, FDA-cleared in the US).8
  • FDA-cleared brands such as iRestore, HairMax and Capillus are sold through importers. iRestore is listed on Flipkart.41 Expect a large one-time cost and buy from authorised sellers so the warranty works.
  • Look for ~650–680 nm red light, real laser diodes (not only LEDs), and full-scalp coverage. Follow the device schedule; trials commonly used 15–25 minutes every other day for 4–6 months.
  • Cheap no-name "laser combs" on marketplaces usually have no clearance or published data.

Korean-style home techniques

Korean scalp care treats the scalp like facial skin: cleanse well, exfoliate gently, add a leave-on treatment, and massage. Here's a version you can do at home.

  1. Wash in the evening. Korean scalp-care advice favours washing at night so sebum, sweat, sunscreen and city dust don't sit on the scalp overnight. Use lukewarm water and rinse for a minute before shampooing.
  2. Lather the scalp, not the hair, and wait 2–3 minutes. Use fingertips, not nails. Contact time is when actives like zinc pyrithione or ketoconazole work. Rinse thoroughly and put conditioner on the lengths only.
  3. Dry the scalp fully. Use a towel blot, then a cool or low-heat dryer. A scalp left damp under a helmet or cap encourages dandruff yeast.
  4. Scale once a week (oily or flaky scalps). Use a salicylic-acid tonic or scaling shampoo (e.g. Aromatica, Dr.FORHAIR scaler). Skip it if your scalp is red or sore.
  5. Apply a tonic or serum to the dry scalp. Part your hair in lines, apply along the thinning zones, and massage it in. Introduce one new product at a time, two weeks apart, so you can spot irritation.
  6. Do a 4-minute scalp massage daily. In a small Japanese study, 9 men who massaged 4 minutes a day for 24 weeks had thicker hair (0.085 → 0.092 mm), though hair counts did not rise.22 In a survey of people with AGA, 69% of those doing longer daily massages reported stabilisation or regrowth, but that is self-report only.23 Press and stretch the scalp in small circles; don't rub the hair.
  7. Use ketoconazole 2% shampoo 2–3 times a week (see the pharmacy section), leaving it on for 3–5 minutes.
  8. Use LLLT on schedule if you buy a device. It suits the evening, before or after the shower on a dry scalp.
  9. Microneedling, carefully. At home, use only shallow rollers (≈0.5 mm), sterilised and never shared. The 1.0–1.5 mm sessions used in trials (e.g. monthly, until pinpoint bleeding) belong in a clinic.13 Skip alcohol or menthol tonics for 24 hours afterwards.
  10. Look after the basics. Get enough protein and sleep. Avoid crash diets, because rapid weight loss (including on GLP-1 drugs like Mounjaro) is linked to shedding.5 If shedding is sudden or diffuse, ask a doctor to check iron (ferritin), vitamin D and thyroid. Supplements help only if you are actually deficient.

Sample routines

Pick the level that fits your budget. Stick with it for at least 6 months before judging. Add one product at a time.

🌱 Level 1: Starter (low cost)

  • Shampoo: a Korean functional shampoo (TS, Dr.FORHAIR, Ryo or Dr.Groot) on most days, and ketoconazole 2% on 2–3 days a week.
  • Leave-on: one serum or tonic once a day on a dry scalp, e.g. Minimalist 18%, Bontress Pro+, or Alpecin Caffeine Liquid.
  • Daily: 4-minute scalp massage.
  • Weekly: one scalp-scaling session if your scalp is oily.

🌷 Level 2: Committed

  • Everything in Level 1, plus
  • An FDA- or MFDS-cleared LLLT helmet on the device's schedule (often every other day).
  • Optional: pumpkin seed oil 400 mg/day, after checking with your doctor.
  • Optional: swap your tonic for a Korean caffeine or exosome ampoule if you enjoy the ritual.

🌺 Level 3: Clinic boost

  • Everything in Level 2, plus
  • A dermatologist-led series of PRP or microneedling (3–6 monthly sessions, then maintenance), with a request for no minoxidil.
  • Exosome therapy only if you accept that it's experimental and you know the exact product.

💡 Stacking tips

  • Don't layer three alcohol-based tonics. One leave-on product is usually enough.
  • Apply serums after LLLT, not before, so the scalp is clean and dry under the light.
  • On microneedling days, skip menthol and alcohol tonics for 24 hours.
  • If your scalp itches, burns or flakes more, stop the newest product.

Track your progress & know the red flags

📏 How to track honestly

  • Take photos on the first Sunday of each month in the same spot, light and hair state (dry, no product). Shoot the hairline from the front and both temples, plus the crown from above.
  • Compare month 0 with month 6, not week to week. Hair grows about 1 cm a month, and new hairs start out thin.
  • Some shedding in the first 1–2 months of a new routine can happen. Losing up to about 100 hairs a day is normal.
  • A dermatologist can do trichoscopy (scalp dermoscopy) to confirm androgenetic alopecia and measure miniaturisation. This gives you a baseline.

🚩 See a dermatologist soon if…

  • You have round, smooth bald patches, or you lose beard or eyebrow hair (possible alopecia areata).
  • Your scalp is painful, burning, scaly-red, or has shiny scar-like areas (scarring alopecias need early treatment).
  • Sudden heavy shedding all over starts 2–3 months after a fever, illness, surgery, crash diet or a new medicine (telogen effluvium).
  • You've followed a routine for 6–12 months and the hairline or crown is still visibly receding. At that point it's worth a fresh conversation about every option, including medical ones.
